# English for Work App — Project Overview

> **For Claude:** This is an overview of what I want to achieve, not a build spec. Read it fully, then follow **Section 10 (How to work with me)**. Your first job is to propose a **proven content plan** before we write any code.

---

## 1. The goal

Build an **Android app** that teaches **Spanish and Portuguese speakers in the US the English they need for their job**, and grow it to about **$4,000/month** in revenue.

- Android first (iOS later, if it works)
- Built with Claude Code
- One strong app, improved over time — not many copy apps

**Working name:** to be decided (Claude can suggest names; avoid names of existing books, apps or channels).

---

## 2. Who it's for

- Spanish speakers first, Portuguese speakers second, living and working in the US
- Hourly workers and job seekers in hands-on jobs
- Beginner to low-intermediate English
- Study on their phone, in short moments: breaks, the bus, before a shift
- Many are price-sensitive; some may have limited reading comfort, so audio and simple design matter

**Haitian Creole is out of scope for now** (I decided the market is too small to start).

---

## 3. The problem

General apps like Duolingo teach general English ("the apple is red"). Workers need **the exact English of their job**: understanding a supervisor, safety warnings, asking for help, talking to customers, filling in forms, a job interview. Today they rely on YouTube videos, Google Translate and phrasebooks.

---

## 4. What the app should do (core ideas)

1. **Choose your job** → learn only the English used in that job
2. **Learn in your own language** — explanations in Spanish or Portuguese
3. **Audio for everything**, plus easy phonetic spelling written the way a Spanish/Portuguese speaker would read it
4. **Speaking practice with AI** — realistic job situations ("Your supervisor says the truck is late. Answer him.")
5. **Short lessons** (about 5 minutes)
6. **Works offline** for lessons already downloaded
7. **Review system** so learners don't forget what they learned

## 6. Money

- Free first lessons so people can try it
- Low price: around **$4.99/month**, or a one-time unlock per industry pack
- **One app with language packs and industry packs** (not separate apps per job/language — safer with Google Play's repetitive-content policy and builds one set of reviews)
- AI speaking practice costs money per use — pricing must cover it

---

## 7. Marketing direction

- Short videos (TikTok, Instagram Reels, YouTube Shorts, Facebook Reels): one useful work phrase per video
- Proof this works: a workplace-English book for Spanish speakers (*Inglés Pa'l Trabajo*, 2026) is tied to a YouTube channel with nearly 2 million views
- Play Store listing in Spanish and Portuguese

---

## 8. What the research found (October 2026)

**Direct competitors — weak:**
- *Lingua Linkup* (iPhone only): industry English/Spanish lessons (construction, hospitality) — 0 ratings, no traction
- No strong Android app teaching job-specific English in the learner's language

**Indirect competitors:**
- *EnGen (Voxy)*: career-focused English by industry, but sold to employers and schools, not directly to workers
- *Duolingo*: general English, not job-focused
- *EngVarta*: live English practice with experts (4.5★, 10,000+ reviews), general conversation
- Community colleges run "Workplace English" programs for employers

**The real competition is free:** YouTube, Google Translate, ChatGPT, phrasebooks. The app must clearly beat "free" with job-specific lessons, audio, speaking practice and a clear learning path.

---

## 9. Rules and guardrails

- **Original content only.** Do not copy lessons, phrases lists or audio from books, apps or channels.
- **Accuracy matters most for safety phrases** (warnings, emergencies, chemicals, lifting, equipment). These must be checked carefully.
- **Natural, US English** as actually spoken at work, plus correct, natural Spanish and Portuguese explanations (not machine-translated feeling).
- **Privacy:** be careful with voice recordings from speaking practice; collect as little personal data as possible.
- **Google Play:** a new personal developer account needs a closed test with 12 testers for 14 days before launch; an organization (LLC) account skips this. Keep this in mind for planning.
