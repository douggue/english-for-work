# Content Style Guide — Spanish → English (v1)

Rules every lesson follows. Writers, reviewers and Claude all use this file.

---

## 1. Spanish (explanations, instructions, meanings)

- **Neutral Latin American Spanish, with *tú*.** Friendly and respectful: "Escucha y repite", "Tu supervisor te dice…".
- **Short sentences.** One idea per sentence. Many learners read slowly.
- **Avoid regional words** that change meaning between countries. Example: *camión* = bus in Mexico, truck elsewhere → write *tráiler* / *camión de carga* and show the English word.
- **Work words people already use** in Spanglish are fine, with the Spanish in parentheses: *el forklift (montacargas)*, *el break (descanso)*.
- **Gender:** write both forms when it matters (*nuevo/nueva*), or rephrase to avoid it (*Es tu primer día*).
- **Never sound machine-translated.** If a native speaker would not say it, rewrite it.

## 2. English (what learners learn)

- **Natural US workplace English**, as people really say it on the job. Use contractions (*I'm, don't, what's*).
- **Level:** Level 0–1 chunks ≤ 8 words; Level 2 up to ~12 words.
- **Teach chunks, not grammar.** A short grammar note only when it fixes a common mistake (e.g. *years **of** experience*).
- **Common spoken forms** are taught for listening even if informal (*Gotta go*, *Heads up!*).
- **No idioms** unless workers hear them daily on the job.

## 3. Phonetic spelling ("Sounds like")

Written so a Spanish speaker reads it close to the real sound. Shown under every English chunk.

| Rule | English | Sounds like |
|---|---|---|
| Accent mark on the **stressed vowel** of each word that carries stress; small unstressed words (*to, a, the, is, it, in, on, for, do*) get no accent | Nice to meet you | náis tu miit iú |
| **h** → **j** | Hi / Who | jái / jú |
| **w** → **u** | Watch out / Where | uách áut / uér |
| **y** sound → **i** | you | iú |
| **Long vowel** (ee) → **ii** — short vowel stays single (helps *ship / sheep*) | meet / ship / sheep | miit / ship / shiip |
| **o** in *job, clock, got* (open "a" sound) → **a** | job / clock | yab / clak |
| **j / dg** sound → **y** at the start, **ch** at the end | job / badge | yab / bach |
| **sh** stays **sh** (+ tip: softer than *ch*, like "shhh") | shift | shift |
| **th** stays **th** (+ tip: tongue between the teeth); in *the, this, that* → **d** | three / this | thrii / dis |
| **v** stays **v** (+ tip: top teeth on the lower lip) | very | véri |
| **t between vowels** (American flap) → **r** | Got it? / water | gárit / uárer |
| **s + consonant at the start**: never add an "e" | school / stop | skul / stap |
| Unstressed *-er, -or* endings → **er** | supervisor | súper-vai-ser |
| **Final consonants are always written** — and always pronounced | first / break | férst / bréik |
| Long words: hyphens between syllables | supervisor | súper-vai-ser |

**Rule of thumb:** the phonetic line is a bridge, not the goal. The audio is always the real model, and every lesson says "escucha el audio".

## 4. Lesson template

Every lesson (~5 min) has these parts, in this order:

| # | Part | Content |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Situation** (es) | 1–2 sentences setting the scene |
| 2 | **Listen first** | Short dialogue (2–6 lines), played twice, then 1 comprehension question with picture or Spanish answers |
| 3 | **Key chunks** | 3–5 chunks: English · sounds like · Spanish · audio (normal + slow) · safety flag yes/no |
| 4 | **Tip** (es) | One pronunciation or usage tip, max 2 sentences |
| 5 | **Practice** | 3–5 exercises: listen → pick meaning; build from tiles; fill the gap |
| 6 | **Shadow** | Repeat each chunk after the audio |
| 7 | **Mini task** | Supervisor/customer says a line → learner picks or says the right reply |
| 8 | **Wrap-up** | Chunks added to review; can-do progress |

Each **unit** = 4 lessons + 1 unit task (AI role-play) + one can-do statement in Spanish (*Puedo…*).

## 5. Safety content

- Every chunk about danger, injury, chemicals, lifting, equipment or emergencies is flagged **safety**.
- Safety chunks must be:
  1. checked against official OSHA wording where it exists (note the source),
  2. approved by someone who has worked that job,
  3. reviewed more often in the review system.
- When in doubt, use the **shortest, most common** warning (*Stop! Watch out! Call 911!*).

## 6. Review checklist (for each lesson)

- [ ] English sounds natural to a native US speaker who has worked this kind of job
- [ ] Spanish is natural, neutral, uses *tú*, no regional traps
- [ ] Phonetic line follows section 3
- [ ] Safety chunks flagged and checked (section 5)
- [ ] Content is original (not copied from books, apps or videos)
- [ ] Lesson fits in ~5 minutes
